HOW IT WORKS

Six stages, one dossier

The agent mirrors how a careful research team actually works a variant — including the part where the team argues with itself before signing anything.

01

Plan

Reads the variant and lays out the investigation before touching any data — what to gather, what would count, what would not.

02

Gather

Pulls cited records only: the frozen ClinVar record, gnomAD population frequencies, and the literature trail for the variant.

03

Compute

Writes its own small analyses and runs them in a no-network sandbox — percentiles, predictor agreement, population context.

04

Judge

Weighs the evidence with an isotonic-calibrated model and the cite-or-decline bar: below three independent items, it refuses.

05

Review

An adversarial second opinion checks independence and calibration, and can downgrade the verdict's confidence — never upgrade it.

06

Design

Proposes the validation experiments that would settle the variant, with the deciding results stated in advance.

BENCHMARK · 01 — VERDICTS

Verdicts on 300 benchmark variants

The agent concludes only when the evidence clears its bar — and declines the rest.

Concluded111 · 37.0%
Declined (evidence below the bar)189 · 63.0%

The agent concludes only where calibrated support exists. Every one of the 189 declines was protective: forcing a verdict from its own underlying scores would not have produced a single extra correct answer (decline precision 1.000). All 900 sandboxed analyses across the 300 runs also completed — 0 failed, 0 timed out.

BENCHMARK · 03 — CALIBRATION

Calibration — do the probabilities mean what they say?

Expected calibration error and Brier score of the reported probabilities. Lower is better on both; bar lengths are scaled to 0.10.

ECE · concluded subset (n = 111)0.0513
ECE · full test set (n = 10,357)0.0248
Brier · concluded subset0.0833

The benchmark subset holds only 111 scored variants, so its calibration runs noisier than the model’s full test set — the gap is consistent with sampling noise, not with the probabilities misbehaving inside the agent.

BENCHMARK · 04 — REVIEWER, HONEST WEAKNESS

Reviewer behaviour — the weakness, published as-is

How the adversarial reviewer treated the 111 concluded verdicts.

Received a material critique100% · 111 of 111
Confidence downgraded93.7% · 104 of 111
Downgraded when the verdict was correct97.0% · 96 of 99
Downgraded when the verdict was wrong66.7% · 8 of 12

The reviewer’s strength challenge fires on nearly every model verdict, because a model verdict’s support usually traces to one correlated predictor family — so it downgrades correct conclusions (97.0%) even more often than wrong ones (66.7%). Its measured value is the audit trail in every dossier, not error detection.

WHY OMEVRA

Built to be believed

Six design rules keep the agent honest. They are also why its benchmark publishes a weakness panel next to the wins.

Temporal integrity

Evidence comes only from records frozen at prediction time (ClinVar, January 2024). The agent never trains on the future — and never peeks at it.

Cite-or-decline bar

Fewer than three independent evidence items and the agent refuses to conclude. The refusal is a first-class result, not a failure mode.

Sandboxed self-analysis

The agent writes its own analysis code and runs it in isolation: no network, a 30-second timeout, and failures shown in the open.

Independent self-review

A separate adversarial stage audits every verdict. It can downgrade confidence language; it can never upgrade a verdict or reverse a decline.

Calibrated probabilities

Reported probabilities are isotonic-calibrated on a temporal test set, so when the agent says 0.99, the number is meant to be believed.

Auditable dossiers

Every verdict ships with a full dossier: each claim cited to its record, each analysis reproducible, each downgrade explained.
